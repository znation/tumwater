### `test/supervisor.test.ts` "startParentDeathWatch takes a SIGKILLed supervisor's grandchild down with it" flaked again on 2026-10-16, after its 10-01 and 10-09 Fixed entries and the 10-11 rewrite to a SIGUSR2-driven poll (found by operator session 2026-10-16 from improve's red merge check)

- **Symptom:** at 07:29:28 PDT, improve's in-lock merge check for `d4b62873` failed one test: `✖ startParentDeathWatch takes a SIGKILLed supervisor's grandchild down with it — AssertionError [ERR_ASSERTION]: the orphaned grandchild's watch fired and ran its stop path`. The check ran 145 s. Recovery re-landed it green at 07:40, and it merged. That is the same assertion as the Fixed entry "The SIGKILL-grandchild supervisor test flaked twice in one 2026-10-09 window". Since then 4ec5d729 (10-11) replaced the real poll with one driven by SIGUSR2 from the test, and its comment says a loaded host "cannot stretch" the verdict. It still fails.
- **Reproduce:** no repro; one live failure under the team's normal load.
- **Cause:** unknown. After SIGKILLing `middle`, the test sends SIGUSR2 up to 200 times, 50 ms apart (about 10 s), and breaks out of the loop early if `process.kill(grandchildPid, …)` throws. The failure reads the same in two cases, and the message cannot tell them apart:
  - the reparent was not visible to the grandchild's `process.ppid` within those signals;
  - the grandchild had already exited without writing `gone`, so the loop broke on ESRCH.
- **Expected / fix sketch:** first make the failure name its case. Record whether the loop broke on ESRCH and how many signals were sent. Have the grandchild write the `ppid()` it saw on each poll. Then fix the case that fires. Re-check the 10-09 entry's mechanism, a watch armed against the wrong baseline, on the handed-down `expectedPpid` path.
- **Progress 2026-10-16 (bugfix):** the test's failure message now names its case: signals sent, whether the loop broke on ESRCH, the expected ppid, and the ppid the grandchild saw on each poll (a `gone.seen` file). The cause is still unknown, so the entry stays open until the next flake reads out.
- **Progress 2026-10-16 (bugfix, second):** one candidate cause closed, unconfirmed: the test treated the existence of `started` as ready, but the grandchild created it with `writeFileSync`, so the test could read an empty or partial pid and SIGUSR2 the wrong pid (ESRCH, no `gone`). The grandchild now writes `started.tmp` and renames it. No flake has been seen since, so the entry stays open until the next failure reads out.
- **Progress 2026-10-16 (bugfix, third):** no repro. 30 concurrent runs of the test beside 8 CPU-spinning node processes all passed (the `timeout` wrapper on the spinners did not exist on this host, so the spinners ran unbounded until `pkill`; the load was real). `startParentDeathWatch` (src/process/supervisor.ts:79-104) reads correctly on the handed-down `expectedPpid` path. No code change; the entry stays open until a flake's failure message (signals sent, ESRCH or not, ppids seen) names the case.
- **Progress 2026-10-16 (bugfix, fourth):** one more candidate closed, unconfirmed: the signal loop was bounded by a count (200 × 50 ms), so a grandchild starved for longer than that (the 145 s check suggests heavy load) could miss every poll and the verdict was read right after. The loop is now bounded by 60 s of wall clock. The entry stays open until a flake reads out.

**Waiting for the next failure 2026-10-16 by bugfix: the failure message will show signals sent, whether the loop broke on ESRCH, the expected ppid, and the ppid the grandchild saw on each poll (`gone.seen`).**

### The phase cell reads "due — starts on the next poll" for two runners the poll just declined and will decline again: the multi-instance cap and the yield-scaled gap record no `skipReason` (found by operator session 2026-10-21 from the GUI's Due chip, `rolePhase` in src/ui/status-model.ts and `phaseInfo` in src/ui/gui/gui-client-model.ts)

- **Symptom:** `rolePhase` renders the plain `due` cell whenever `nextRunAt <= now` and `skipReason` names none of its five values (src/ui/status-model.ts:283-284, the `default:` arm), and `phaseInfo` renders that as "Due — starts on the next poll" (src/ui/gui/gui-client-model.ts:56). Two scheduling-pass declinations land a runner there although the poll has already decided against it and the same cause holds on the next pass, so the cell promises a start that does not come for the length of the episode:
  1. **The multi-instance cap** (src/orchestrator/orchestrator-scheduling.ts:386-388): `noteSkip(root, runner, undefined)` once the base role's count of instances already writing has reached `instances`. Reachable with an in-range idle sibling: `status-roles.ts:66` gives an idle runner a row only while `instanceIndex <= configuredInstances`, and at `instances: 2` the pool is `feature`, `feature-2`, `feature-3`, `feature-4` (src/roles/runner-ids.ts:52-55, twice the instance count), so instances 1 and 3 writing leaves instance 2 idle, listed, and reading `due` although its two-instance cap is full — for as long as one of those two runs.
  2. **The yield-scaled gap** (src/scheduling/scheduling.ts:123, `if (sinceLast < minGap && !woken) return { run: false }`, reached as the not-due arm at src/orchestrator/orchestrator-scheduling.ts:365). `scheduleAtMinInterval` writes `nextRunAt = now + minRunIntervalSeconds * 1000` with no multiplier (src/scheduling/backoff.ts:133-135), while the gate multiplies (src/scheduling/scheduling.ts:106, :116 — `minGap = minGapSeconds * 1000 * mult`). A scalable role (src/roles/roles.ts:77) whose multiplier is above 1 therefore passes its persisted `nextRunAt` and then waits out the stretched gap with no reason recorded — for the whole gap, not one cycle. At `mult` 8 (`yieldMultiplier`'s cap) the steward's 6 h default gap (src/config/config.ts:50) becomes 48 h, of which the last 42 h read `due`; a 30-minute role reads `due` for 3.5 h.
- **Reproduce:** deterministic, no model, no fixture for the first; the second is a scenario.
  - Yield gap (verified offline against `dist/`): `isEligible({ role: "steward", config: { roles: { steward: { enabled: true, minRunIntervalSeconds: 21600 } } }, state: { role: "steward", runs: 5, lastRunEndedAt: now - 6*3600_000, nextRunAt: now, backoffSeconds: 0, recentOutcomes: "n".repeat(20), lastMainHead: "abc", lastResult: "no_change", consecutiveErrors: 0 } }, now, "abc", 0)` returns `{ run: false }` **with no `reason`** — so the pass calls `noteSkip(..., undefined)` — while `yieldMultiplierFor` reads 8 and `rolePhase(state, true)` returns `"due"`. The same state at `now + 42 h` is eligible (`{ run: true, reason: "scheduled" }`), so the `due` cell stood for 42 h. A control state with `nextRunAt = now + 60_000` reads `"sleeping (for 1m)"`, which is what the pre-clock case must keep doing.
  - Instances cap: `instances: 2` with instances 1 and 3 writing and instance 2 idle — `feature-2` has a row, is declined at :387, and reads `due`.
- **Cause:** `noteSkip(root, runner, undefined)` means "no reason recorded", and the phase model's `default:` arm reads exactly that as the plain due state, whose meaning is "starts on the next poll". Both paths are real declinations whose cause outlives the poll, so a one-poll label cannot describe them. In (2) the phase also reads a different clock than the gate: `nextRunAt` is persisted without the yield multiplier, and the multiplier is the only thing holding the run back — the `×N` suffix lives on the next-run cell (`status-render.ts`'s `nextRunCell` via `yieldMultiplierSuffix`, src/scheduling/backoff.ts:109), so the two cells of one row disagree.
- **Expected / fix sketch:** record the reason at both sites and teach both phase twins the new values; test/gui-client.test.ts pins the two copies against each other, so they change together.
  - src/runner/runner-state.ts: extend the `SkipReason` union (:332) and `SKIP_REASONS` (:334) with `instances-full` and `yield-gap`.
  - src/orchestrator/orchestrator-scheduling.ts: pass `"instances-full"` at :387 and `"yield-gap"` in the not-due arm (:365) — the latter only when the runner is due by its own clock but held by the gap (`now >= s.nextRunAt`), so an ordinary pre-clock sleep keeps its absent reason and its `sleeping (for …)` cell.
  - src/ui/status-model.ts `rolePhase`: `instances-full` → render it with the `claim-hold` wording (`idle: another ${baseRoleOf(s.role)} instance is working on it`, :272) — `rolePhase` sees only `RunnerState`, which carries neither the configured `instances` nor the live writing count, so a count-bearing label would need a snapshot-computed param like `maintenanceQuotaHold`; the sibling wording is the same advice with no new plumbing. `yield-gap` → `waiting out its gap ×${mult}` built from `yieldMultiplierFor`/`yieldMultiplierSuffix` (src/scheduling/backoff.ts:98, :109), which the state does provide, so the phase agrees with the next-run cell instead of contradicting it.
  - src/ui/gui/gui-client-model.ts `phaseInfo` keys off the *rendered string*, not the skip reason: `idle: ` is already matched (:54, gray), and `waiting out its gap ×N` matches no existing prefix, so it needs its own branch (gray, detail `stretched by N×`) or it falls through to the `other` chip.
  - docs/glossary.md:33: narrow the `due` row — it currently reads "A role is ready and starts on the next poll" — to the states that really start on the next pass, and name what the new labels mean.
  - Tests: test/status-model.test.ts (both labels from state, and the pre-clock `sleeping` cell unchanged), test/gui-client.test.ts (the two twins agree), test/scheduling.test.ts (the yield-gap `isEligible` case returns no reason), test/orchestrator-scheduling.test.ts (a poll declined by the cap records `instances-full`); `npm test` green.
- **Considered and excluded:** the maintenance allowance's admission (src/orchestrator/orchestrator-scheduling.ts:453) also calls `noteSkip(..., undefined)`, but its headroom is recomputed from in-flight roles every pass (`maintenanceQuotaOverLine` at :259-262 owns the "used has reached the allowance" case, which already reads `waiting: maintenance allowance used …` through `maintenanceQuotaHold`), so a role declined only by the one-poll reservation is normally admitted on the next pass and the label's promise holds there. Left alone rather than adding a third state for a promise that keeps itself.
- **Out of scope, deliberately:** the 2 s poll and the start latency it costs stay as they are — this entry makes the label tell the truth, it does not add a wake mechanism. The multiplier/clock split stays too, since `yieldMultiplierSuffix`'s own comment already declares a `now`-due cell at ×N to be "a role waiting out its gap, not a broken clock"; only the phase cell contradicts it.

### `three T-long reviews run at once at cap 4, so all three merge in about T, not 3T` failed, then passed on the same tree (quarantined by tumwater 2026-10-23)

~~~~
three T-long reviews run at once at cap 4, so all three merge in about T, not 3T
~~~~

**Progress 2026-10-23 by bugfix: not reproduced in ~25 runs — alone, under four-way and merge-heavy parallel load, and one full 7090-test suite run (all green). The test's three failure assertions already name their cause: `one review per change`, `all three reviews overlapped (in flight at each start: <samples>)`, and the span check `review to last merge took <X> ms, no less than the reviews' <Y> ms sum`. Two candidate paths, both driven by full-suite load: (a) a review's 15 s shell-loop wait (300 × 0.05 s) for three in-flight run files times out, so that review's sample counts < 3 and overlap is reported missed; (b) the span bound's floor (the measured review_verdict durationMs sum) excludes the three serialized git merges, so a loaded host's merge time pushes `lastMerged - firstStart` past that floor even when the reviews did overlap. The samples mechanism already forces overlap deterministically for staggered starts, so (a) needs a >15 s start gap and (b) needs > ~4 s of merge time — both plausible only under the full suite's contention.**

**Waiting for the next failure 2026-10-23 by bugfix: which of the three assertions fires and its numbers — the overlap assertion's `in flight at each start:` list (which sample is < 3), the `one review per change` count (extra samples), or the span check's two ms figures**
